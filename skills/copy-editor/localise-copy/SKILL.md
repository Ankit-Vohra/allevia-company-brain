## Method

1. Find the brand's language rule first: `playbook` (topic "language" and kind "voice"). Many Indian brands post the main line in simple Hindi and the same facts in plain English, because people search in both. `search` two of the brand's own posts to see how it mixes them.
2. Apply the stranger test in every language: a reader who knows nothing about the business understands each line on its own. Name the object ("the job application", not "the application"); never leave a verb without its object or a count without its noun; no verbless fragments ("Directly to the company" to do what?).
3. Write the way people speak, not the way documents read: everyday Hindi, not literary Hindi; English nouns people really use (job, app, delivery, booking) stay English. Respectful register in captions and on images (aap, kijiye); characters in a reel may speak casually to each other.
4. Check words that misfire out of context. A word that also means money next to a price can read as a loan or payment app; "from home, on your phone" reads like a scam ad; "first job free" can read as "your first job is free" when it means the first posting. Put the clarifying noun in the same line.
5. Numbers and money: digits are easier to scan (₹18,000, 2 km); Indian grouping for rupees (1,25,000), the local symbol and format elsewhere (AED 1,500; A$80; £45; $45). Never convert or round an amount. Dates the local way (12 Oct in the UK, India, UAE and Australia; Oct 12 in the US).
6. Spelling follows the reader: UK and Australian spelling (colour, organise, enquiry) or US (color, organize, inquiry). Keep one variant through the whole text, the one the brand already uses.
7. Mark what you cannot judge: a regional word or a line you are not sure sounds natural is quoted and flagged, not rewritten into something stiff.
8. Verdict: ok, or fix with each line quoted and its localised version; the facts never change.

## House style

One line per change: the line, what misreads, the fix. Both languages shown together when the brand posts both.

> "पहली नौकरी मुफ़्त" reads as "your first job is free". Write "पहली वैकेंसी मुफ़्त डालिए / Post your first vacancy free".
> "₹150000" is hard to read. Write ₹1,50,000.
> "organize" in a UK brand's post. Write "organise".

## Done well

- [ ] Every line passes the stranger test in each language.
- [ ] The brand's language order and register are kept.
- [ ] Words that misfire next to money or work were caught.
- [ ] Numbers, currency, dates and spelling fit the reader's market, with no amount changed.

## Common mistakes

- Translating word for word, which gives a notice instead of a post.
- Literary Hindi a working reader stumbles over.
- Mixing US and UK spelling in one post.
- Changing a price or a date while localising it.
