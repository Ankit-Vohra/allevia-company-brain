## Method

1. Read the brand first. `playbook` with kind "voice" gives the brand's own rules and the rules learned from the owner's edits; they win over every pattern below. If the brand writes with dashes or exclamation marks on purpose, keep them at its rate. If the rules are thin, `search` for two of the brand's own posts and match their sentence length and words.
2. Read the whole text once and mark the tells, strongest first. A. Staging: "not just X, but Y", "it's not X, it's Y", "this isn't about X" when nobody said it was; one-line closers that repeat the point ("That's the real win"); deep-sounding sayings ("at its core"); run-ups ("Let's dive in", "Here's what you need to know", "Honestly?"); arguing with an objection nobody raised. B. Rhythm by rule: three items every time, the same sentence opening three times, a dash in every sentence, stacked hedges ("could potentially"). C. Inflation: stock words (pivotal, testament, landscape, delve, seamless, vibrant, unlock, elevate), "plays a key role", "the future looks bright", "serves as" and "boasts" for "is" and "has", unnamed experts, sales words (nestled, stunning, breathtaking). D. Formatting: bold on every label, emojis or arrows as decoration, a heading repeated in the first line. E. Leftovers: "Great question!", "I hope this helps", "let me know if", "as of my last update", text about the text ("the table below shows"). F. Wrong reader: a reply that re-explains what the owner already knows before reaching the decision.
3. One sighting of an A or E tell is enough to edit. A B, C or D tell needs company: one dash or one "crucial" in a plain paragraph is fine.
4. Edit wording only. State the point directly; cut the wrapper and keep the content; turn a dash into a comma, a colon or a full stop; replace a stock word with the plain one ("crucial" becomes "important", or just go); merge a forced triad into the items that are really different. Never add a fact, number, name, date, claim or source, and never drop one, so Nisha's check still passes after your edit. If a sentence needs a detail you do not have, write a simpler sentence.
5. Keep the shape. The answer's first line, its numbered steps, its five-item limit and its one next action stay exactly where they are (the answer shape wins over voice). Do not merge list items or add a closer.
6. Match the owner's market. Spell the way the question and the brand spell (colour or color, organise or organize, enquiry or inquiry), write dates the local way (12 Oct; US readers may expect Oct 12), keep amounts in the currency given (AED 1,500; ₹1,25,000; A$80). In Hindi or Hinglish, the same tells exist: "सिर्फ़ X नहीं, बल्कि Y", "आइए जानते हैं", "उम्मीद है यह जानकारी मददगार रही"; keep the brand's register (see the Hindi and Hinglish skill).
7. As a reviewer, give the verdict: ok when no strong tell is left; fix with each phrase quoted and its plain replacement; reject only when nothing is left once the padding is gone.

## House style

The edit reads like the owner talking to a customer: short and long sentences mixed, plain verbs, the specific detail kept. The note lists each change as the quote and its replacement, at most five, strongest first.

> "It's not just a bakery, it's a family tradition" becomes "We have baked here since 1998, three generations so far." (only if the record says so; otherwise "We have baked here for three generations" is cut back to what the record holds)
> "Great question! Let's dive in. Sign-ups fell from 58 to 41." becomes "Sign-ups fell from 58 to 41."
> "Our team — small but mighty — loves helping you!" becomes "Our team of four answers every message the same day." (with the four and the same day from the record), or simply "Our team answers every message."

## Done well

- [ ] No "not just X, but Y", no chatbot wrapper, no staged opener and no repeating closer is left.
- [ ] Every number, name, date, amount, source and "waiting for your OK" is exactly as before.
- [ ] The first line, the list items and the one next action are where they were.
- [ ] The brand's own voice rules and the owner's edits were followed over any pattern here.
- [ ] Spelling, dates and currency fit the owner's market, and Hindi keeps the brand's register.

## Common mistakes

- Swapping one stock word for another ("elevate" becomes "enhance").
- Adding a vivid detail to make it sound human: a made-up fact is worse than a dull sentence.
- Flattening the brand's real voice: if the owner writes warmly with an exclamation mark, that is their voice, not a tell.
- Rewriting the structure of an answer, so the first line is no longer the answer.
- Treating every list of three as forced: three real items are fine.
