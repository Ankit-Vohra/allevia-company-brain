## Method

1. Learn the voice before touching a word. `playbook` with topic "voice" (and the channel, if known) gives this brand's own rules; they come before everything below. `search` and `get_entity` on the product, service or offer the draft is about give the facts you may use. Keep the writer's message: you improve it, you do not replace it.
2. Edit in seven passes, one thing at a time. After each pass, re-read the earlier ones quickly so a later change did not break them.
   1. Clear: can a busy reader on a phone say what this is and what to do? Cut sentences that try to say two things; swap trade words for the customer's.
   2. Voice: does it sound like this brand all the way through (no switch from warm to corporate halfway)?
   3. So what: after each claim, ask "so what?" and add the reason the customer cares ("hand-finished seams, so the hem never drops").
   4. Prove it: every "best", "loved", "trusted" needs proof from the records (a named review, a count with its date, a real photo). With no proof, soften the claim or cut it; never invent one.
   5. Specific: "quick" becomes "ready in 10 days"; "many happy clients" becomes the real count, or goes. Never write a number the records do not hold; mark it "[you tell us: how many clients last year]".
   6. Feeling: one real moment the customer will recognise (the wedding photo, the first day in the new office). One, not five.
   7. No risk: answer the doubt next to the action (price or "from" price, how to cancel, how long, what if it does not fit) and make the action plain ("Book a fitting").
3. Strip the tells of machine writing on the way out: "it's not X, it's Y" reveals, "no X, no Y, no Z" lists, "The result? ..." self-answered questions, stock phrases ("unlock", "elevate", "take it to the next level"), engagement bait ("Agree?"), every line its own one-sentence paragraph, emoji as bullets, dashes used as pauses. The remove-ai-writing and make-it-sound-human skills hold the full lists.
4. Check nothing a reader relies on moved: names, prices, dates, numbers and the call to action must match the original or the records. If an edit changes a fact, undo it.
5. Return the edited text whole, then the three to five biggest changes with a reason each, so the writer learns the pattern.

## House style

The edited draft first, ready to paste, then a short list of what changed and why. Nothing else.

> Your first fitting is 30 minutes, at the studio or on a video call. We take 14 measurements, so the jacket fits the way you stand, not the way a size chart guesses. Most suits are ready in 3 weeks.
> Book a fitting
>
> What changed:
> - "Premium bespoke experience" became what actually happens in the fitting (so what, specific).
> - "Loved by hundreds" went: no count on record (prove it).
> - "Submit" became "Book a fitting" (no risk).

## Done well

- [ ] The brand's own voice rules were read first and win over this method.
- [ ] Each of the seven passes was done in order, one at a time.
- [ ] Every claim left in has proof from the records, or was softened or cut.
- [ ] No number, name, price or date was added or changed without a source.
- [ ] The machine-writing tells are gone.
- [ ] The edit comes back whole, with the biggest changes explained.

## Common mistakes

- Rewriting the message instead of improving it.
- Making it "more specific" by inventing numbers.
- Piling on emotion until it reads like an advert from 1995.
- Fixing everything in one pass and missing the unproven claims.
- Returning only notes, so the writer still has to do the edit.
