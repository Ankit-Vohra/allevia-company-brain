## Method

1. Check what is already on record first: `search` for the rival's name (type Competitor). A reading from the last three days is reused for free; only look again when the owner wants today's picture.
2. Name the rivals with the owner's own words: at most three, each with what is known (Instagram handle, website, Facebook page name). Do not guess a handle: a wrong one reads someone else's account. If none are named, ask for up to three and stop.
3. Run `competitor_watch` once for all of them (public Instagram numbers, ads in Meta's Ad Library, Google ads by website). For one brand's ads only, `ad_library`. For a rival's rating and reviews count, `google_listing` with its name and town.
4. Each lookup costs a few cents from the business's monthly budget. If the tool says lookups are off or the budget is used, say so in one line and answer from what is on record.
5. Read the ads for the move, not the copy: what offer, which button ("Order now", "Book"), how long it has run (an ad running for months is probably working for them), how many are running now. Compare with this business's own ads and offer (`playbook` for its rules).
6. Pick one move for this business, sized in time or money, and say what to watch next month (the next `competitor_watch` reading shows whether the rival kept it up).

## House style

What the rival is doing, what it suggests, one move. Every rival figure says "public" and its date.

> Rival Rolls has run "Two croissants for £3" on Facebook and Instagram since 1 Oct (Meta Ad Library, public, as of 15 Oct), and 1 of its 2 ads is running now. Their followers went from 1,500 to 1,620 in four days. Move: test a weekday bundle in your next post, about 30 minutes. We'll check their ads again next month.

## Done well

- [ ] Rivals were named by the owner, with handles that were checked, three at most.
- [ ] Each figure says it is public and gives its date; a reused reading says when it was read.
- [ ] Nothing is claimed about a rival's spend, reach or results.
- [ ] One move, sized, and what to watch next.
- [ ] An off or used-up budget is said plainly, in one line.

## Common mistakes

- Saying how much a rival spends or how many people saw an ad: the libraries hide that, except for ads shown in the EU or UK and political ads.
- Treating a long-running ad as proof it works: it is a hint, say so.
- Reading a private person's account: only businesses, only public profiles.
- Looking up the same rival again the same day and spending twice.
- Copying a rival's ad text: take the idea, write it in this brand's voice.
