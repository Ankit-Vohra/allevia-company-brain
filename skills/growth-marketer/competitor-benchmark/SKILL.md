## Method

1. Say what you can compare. `playbook` with kind "benchmark" (and the channel) returns industry norms stored for this business; `search` for competitors finds what was recorded about them (their site, public posts, prices). Nothing else is known: you have no access to a competitor's private numbers, so never state them.
2. Take the business's own number for the same thing and period with `metric_trend` (for example Instagram followers, posting rate, average rating), and set it beside the benchmark or the competitor's public figure. Same channel, similar size, same period, or the comparison is unfair.
3. Label every comparison with its source type: "industry norm" (a benchmark, not their own number), "public figure" (as seen on a date), or "their own". Add the as-of date. A competitor figure older than a few months is a hint.
4. Look for the gap that matters to the owner's goal, not every gap: a rival posting 5 times a week when you post once matters only if posting frequency moves your goal.
5. Pick one catch-up move, concrete and sized ("post twice a week for four weeks: about 1 hour a week"), not five.
6. If there is nothing recorded about competitors, say so and say what to connect or add (a list of three rivals), and stop.

## House style

Where you stand, the gap that matters, one move. Source type on every number.

> Your Instagram posts once a week; the three rivals we recorded post 3 to 5 times (their public pages, as of 10 Oct). Your saves per post are higher than the industry norm (benchmark, not your rivals' numbers). Move: post twice a week for four weeks, about 1 hour a week.

## Done well

- [ ] Each comparison says whether it is a norm, a public figure or their own, with a date.
- [ ] Like is compared with like: channel, size, period.
- [ ] No private competitor number is claimed.
- [ ] One move, sized in time.
- [ ] A missing benchmark is said plainly.

## Common mistakes

- Presenting an industry average as the competitor's result.
- Comparing a big brand's followers with a local shop's.
- Listing ten gaps and no move.
- Using a stale competitor price as current.
