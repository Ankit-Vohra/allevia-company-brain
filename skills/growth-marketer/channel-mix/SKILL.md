## Method

1. Start from the goal, not the channel. Use `search` for the owner's goals (what number they want to move) and the channels the business is on. A channel is judged by that number, not by likes.
2. Gather what each channel returns, same period for all: `metric_trend` for each channel's main number (followers, visits, sign-ups from it), `content_performance` for Instagram posts, `seo_report` for search visits and rankings, `ad_report` for paid. Note which channels have no data at all: say "not recorded" and what to connect.
3. Rank by return on effort for a small team, not by size: owned channels (the website and search, email or WhatsApp lists) compound; rented ones (social, paid) stop when you stop. Rule of thumb for a small business: one main channel, one test, everything else on low effort. Five channels at half effort do worse than two done well.
4. For each of the top three channels write: what it gave (a number with its period), the effort it costs (hours a week, in words), and the first move. Include one channel to stop or cut to maintenance, with the reason.
5. Check evidence age. Anything older than 7 days says "as of <date>". Sample or benchmark numbers are labelled.
6. If the evidence is too thin to rank, say that, and recommend one cheap test with a date to judge it.

## House style

A ranked short list of at most three, then what to stop, then one next action. Each line: the channel, what it gave, the move.

> 1. Google search: 310 visits in the week to 12 Oct, 22 enquiries (Search Console and website). Fix the two pages ranking 8 to 15: about 2 hours.
> 2. Instagram: reach is steady, saves are low. Keep two posts a week.
> Stop: the Facebook page (no visits recorded since August).
> Next: pick which two pages to fix first (2 minutes).

## Done well

- [ ] The ranking is tied to the owner's goal number.
- [ ] Every channel line has a number with its period, or "not recorded".
- [ ] Effort is stated in hours, not "some work".
- [ ] One channel is named to stop or cut back.
- [ ] Old or sample data is labelled.

## Common mistakes

- Recommending every channel at once.
- Ranking by follower count rather than by the number the owner wants.
- Treating a channel with no data as a failing channel; it is unknown.
- Advising spend on a channel before the website converts the visits it already gets.
