## Method

0. Purpose first. Before making anything, fill the one-line brief (open the creative-brief skill): the goal (sell, announce, show a feature, get enquiries or bookings, build trust), the one product, service, feature or offer it promotes (by its name in the records), for whom, the one message, the call to action and where it runs. Infer each from the records and the owner's words; ask at most three quick questions with suggested answers only for what matters and is unknown; if the owner says "just make it", pick the strongest product or offer for their goal and say why. Open the piece with the brief in one line.
1. Name the post's one job before writing a word: announce something new, prove the work (a result, a before and after), invite a reply or a visit, or remind people of an offer. A post with two jobs does neither. If the request is vague ("a post about the new menu"), pick the job from the facts in step 4 and say which you picked in one line.
2. Read the brand's rules first. Call `playbook` for the voice rules on Instagram (topic "voice", channel "instagram") and for the hook formulas (topic "hooks", channel "instagram"). Rules the owner taught the team from their edits come back in the same list; treat them as law, above your own habits.
3. See what worked here, not what works in general. Call `content_performance` for Instagram over the last 60 days. Look at the best two posts and the weakest one: copy the hook family (a question, a number, a "before" line), never the words. If fewer than five posts are recorded, say "only a few posts to learn from" and lean on the hook formulas from step 2. When the owner asks what rivals post, or the business has few posts of its own, call `competitor_watch` for the two or three rivals the owner named (with their handles): it returns each rival's three best public posts with likes, comments, views and links, and how often they post. Look for a pattern in two of the three (format, opening line, subject), never one lucky post, and borrow the idea, never the words. Those are public numbers with a date: say so, and never call them reach or saves, which are private to the account. A lookup costs a few cents from the monthly budget; if lookups are off, say it in one line and carry on with this business's own posts.
4. Collect the facts. Call `search` for the project, product or offer named in the request, then `get_entity` on the best one or two hits. Only facts that come back may appear in the caption: names, places, years, materials, prices, results. Where the post needs a fact the record does not hold, write "[you tell us: the fact in a few words]" in that spot. Never invent it, and never drop the whole caption because one detail is missing.
5. Pick the format by the job: one photo for proof or a launch, a carousel (5 to 8 slides, the first one carries the hook) for a process or a list, a reel for reach (hand a reel to the reel script skill instead). Say which format and why in a few words.
6. Write the caption in the language the brand posts in (see the voice rules and audience), even if the owner asked in another language. Shape: a hook line, then two or three short sentences, then one closing line only if the brand's rules allow one.
7. Write one line of alt text (what is in the photo, in plain words) and choose three to five hashtags: one brand tag, two or three about the work or place, none that are generic (#love, #instagood).
8. If the owner asked for a post they can use, hand it over with `propose_action` (kind content_draft, body = the whole caption, the alt text and the hashtags). It becomes a draft waiting for their OK. Ideas and options need no proposal.

## House style

The post is finished text the owner can paste, not a description of one. The first line is the hook and must work alone, because only about the first 125 characters show before "more". The body is two or three sentences a person would say out loud, about 150 to 300 characters in all for a single photo and up to about 500 for a story-style post. Hashtags go on their own line at the end, never inside a sentence. At most one emoji, and none if the voice rules or the owner's instructions say so. Prices and numbers appear exactly as the record has them.

Example of the shape (a bakery, invented for illustration):

> The kitchen window faces east, so the first loaf is out by 6.
> Our sourdough takes 30 hours from starter to oven, and the crust is the part people write to us about. Open from 7 on weekdays.
> #cornerbakery #sourdough #freshbread
> Photo: a sourdough loaf cooling on a wire rack by a window.

Below the caption, tell the owner in one line which hook family you used and why it fits, and list each "[you tell us: …]" spot.

## Done well

- [ ] It has a purpose: the one-line brief (goal, what it promotes, for whom, message, action, where) opens it, and every part serves it; a good-looking piece with no purpose fails.
- [ ] The first line stands alone and makes someone stop; it is not a label, a name or a date.
- [ ] One job, one action: a single closing line, or none, never two asks.
- [ ] Every name, place, year, number and claim is one the record holds; every gap is marked "[you tell us: …]".
- [ ] It sounds like this brand: the voice rules and the owner's own rules are followed word for word.
- [ ] It is written in the language the brand posts in, with three to five specific hashtags on their own line and a line of alt text.
- [ ] The hook belongs to a family that did well here, or the caption says it is a fresh try.

## Common mistakes

- A caption that is only a label ("Project name, place, year."). Nobody would say that out loud.
- Opening with "We are excited to announce" or "Introducing": the audience scrolls past announcements that sound like press releases.
- Inventing a detail to fill a gap (a material, an award, a quote) because the post felt thin. A marked gap is better than a made-up fact.
- Stacking two or three calls to action ("Like, share, follow and DM us").
- Ten or more hashtags, or hashtags that have nothing to do with the photo.
- Copying a winning post's words instead of its hook family, so the feed starts to repeat itself.
