Your business stays yours.

What we do to protect your data, in plain words, and what we do not claim. Everything on this page is something we can point to in how Allevia is built.

The three promises

  1. Each business is its own separate database

    Your business's memory lives in a database of its own, apart from every other business's. Nothing is shared across businesses, and nothing learned from one business is used for another.

  2. Nothing goes out without your OK

    Your team writes drafts and proposes actions. Anything that costs money, speaks in public or changes something waits on a card until you say OK. Nothing is posted by Allevia today.

  3. We keep what we need, and say what that is

    Sensitive details are stripped from text before it is stored or sent to the AI model, photos lose their location, and the privacy policy lists what is collected, why, and who handles it.

How it works underneath.

Separate databases
Each business gets its own graph in FalkorDB, named for the business, plus one small registry that lists the businesses. A request is tied to one business before your team starts, and none of the team's tools can name another business's database. The tool that lets the team look things up freely is read-only: the database itself refuses any change.
Who can open a business
Each business opens only with its own private owner link: a long random secret that is shown once, stored only as a one-way fingerprint, and can be cancelled at once. Without it, the business's pages and photos are refused. It is one shared link for the business, not a personal login, so keep it private. A business marked as a public demo opens without one, read-only.
What businesses do share
Generic rule books that hold no business's information, such as search-engine checks, platform rules and a timeline of Google updates. Every business gets the same copy.
Photos
Each business's photos sit in a folder of its own. A photo is only served if it belongs to the business that asked. The location data inside a photo is removed on arrival, and the stored copy has no hidden metadata. How people in photos are handled is explained in the privacy policy.
Approvals
Posts, replies, issues and anything that spends money are proposed as cards. By default, approving records the decision and saves the draft; it does not publish or post. Replies to reviews of three stars or lower, price and legal claims, and ad spend never go out on their own.
Filtering sensitive details
Before text is stored or sent to the AI model, a filter removes passwords, tokens, account numbers, email addresses and phone numbers, and a scan checks the stored memory for any it missed. It is cautious on purpose: it also removes a business's own public phone number and email.
Read-only connections
When we connect to a system to read your numbers, only a short list of read-only actions is allowed, and anything else is refused before it is sent. The tool that lets another AI program read your memory can read, never write.
Reading your website
The crawler calls itself AlleviaBot, follows your robots.txt, only visits public pages, refuses addresses on private networks and checks every redirect before following it.
The public demo
The public demo is read-only. Every action that would change something is refused, it never calls the AI model, and it replays recorded answers instead of making new ones.
Secrets
Passwords and tokens live in a settings file on the server, outside the code. The AI model's process is given only the few settings it needs, and a test checks every file in the code for anything that looks like a secret or private identifier.

Discord.

Private areas
Each business gets its own private category on our Discord server, with its own channels. A role for that business unlocks only that area. Everyone else, including other customers, sees nothing, and the bot does not answer direct messages.
Which business a message belongs to
The bot works out the business from the channel a message was posted in, using a list kept in the database. Nothing a person types, and no channel name, can choose the business.
Joining
Each person joins with an invite that works once and expires after seven days. If we cannot tell which invite someone used, they get no access until we add them by hand.
Who can decide
Only people with the business's own role can ask its team questions or press Approve and Reject. Our staff role can read the area and answer in #support, but never asks your team questions or decides your cards.
What Discord keeps
Messages you write live on Discord, under Discord's own terms, and we also store the question and the answer in your business's memory. What you write in #support is copied to a staff-only channel so a person can answer it. Discord names and ids are not written into your business's memory.

What we do not claim.

We would rather you know the gaps than find them.

  • We are not SOC 2 audited, and we will not say we are until we are.
  • Your text and photos go to an AI model. To answer, the team sends what it needs to Claude, made by Anthropic. The privacy policy lists every company that handles data for us.
  • There are no personal accounts yet. A business has one owner link, not one login per person: no individual users, no roles and no record of who did what. Real sign-in is on our build list, and this page will change when it ships.
  • The separation is enforced by our software, not by separate database accounts. A mistake in our code could cross it. We test it by trying to break it (forged business names, crafted queries, other businesses' photos and Discord channels), and giving each business its own database login is next on our list.
  • Our people can see your workspace to set it up and to help you. There is not yet a log you can read of when they did.
  • No data-region promise yet. The servers' region is in the privacy policy. We do not offer a choice of region.
  • Deleting and exporting are done by us, on request, not by a button. How to ask.
