# Owner words: how Allevia talks to the people who pay for it

The founder, 2026-10-15 15:10 IST: "who is the target audience of this product. It is founders who are doing good business
but lack knowledge and time to do other stuff which is necessary to take their business from 1 to 100. They have
travelled 0 to 1 and now this can help them to take it to the next leap. Your 90 days, day 0 and all … many things are
very much complicated for someone to understand."

Every screen, message, brief, answer, the marketing site and the demo follow this page. Where it disagrees with an older
spec, this page wins.

## Who we build for

Owners whose business already works: customers, revenue, a reputation. They got from 0 to 1 on their own craft. Getting
to 100 takes marketing, numbers, a better website, content and follow-ups, and they have neither the time nor the
know-how for those. They are not marketers, analysts or engineers. They read Allevia on a phone between jobs, for two
minutes.

**The test:** an owner understands any screen in five seconds, with no training. If something needs explaining, it
belongs behind a "Show how" expander.

## How the setup works: we set it up, you steer

1. **We do the setup.** Onboarding reads the website and whatever accounts are connected. The team then proposes a
   plan: three goals in plain words, each showing "where you are now" and "where we'll get you". The owner taps OK or
   changes a number. That is the whole setup on their side.
2. **The team runs it.** Each month the team checks its own progress, adjusts targets and what it works on, and writes
   one short note. It asks the owner first only when a change costs money, speaks in public or moves a goal.
3. **The owner has two jobs:**
   - say OK or No to what is waiting for them;
   - tell the team things in their own words ("no posts on Sundays", "push the new menu").

The machinery stays exactly as it is: goals measured from real data, a fixed starting point, monthly reviews, learned
rules and access levels. The owner sees what it means, not how it works.

## Words

| Don't say | Say |
|---|---|
| Allevia 90 / the 90-day program | Your growth plan (3 months) |
| Day 0 / baseline / baseline locked | Where you started |
| Day 30 / 60 / 90 checkpoint | Monthly check-in |
| Day N of 90 | Month 1 · week 2 |
| Target / progress 0..1 | Goal: "Get to 40 enquiries a week", with a simple bar |
| On track / at risk / off track | Going well / Needs a push / Behind (with one line on why) |
| No data / no baseline | We'll know after your first full week |
| First-pass rate / first-pass curve | Drafts you approved as they were |
| Learned rules | Things your team learned from your edits |
| Capabilities / access mode / api, public, benchmark | What your team can see · "Connect Instagram to see more" |
| Approval card / pending approval | Waiting for your OK |
| Recommendation / finding / detector / insight | What we noticed · Idea |
| Agent / desk / orchestrator / synthesis | Your team · the person's role name and team ("Miss Influencer · Content Studio") |
| Citations / provenance / graph / node | Sources · "how we know" |
| Simulated | Sample numbers (always said, never hidden) |
| Morning brief | Today's note |
| Deliverables | Posts, emails and fixes your team made |

## Screens

- **Home shows three things:**
  1. One line on how the business is doing this week, with a comparison ("Enquiries up 12% on last week").
  2. What's waiting for your OK.
  3. One next step.

  Everything else is one tap away.
- **At most three numbers per card,** each with a plain comparison. No bare rates, decimals or ratios.
- **The answer and its one next action come first.** Who worked on it, sources, checks and the graph path sit behind
  "Show how we got this".
- **Time is shown in the business's own time zone,** as words ("this morning", "last Tuesday").

## The office

An open-plan floor, not closed rooms. Each team keeps its own zone, marked with a coloured rug, a hanging sign and its
desks grouped together. Low shelves and plants divide the zones, not walls. The cafeteria and the game room are open
corners of the same floor. A glass meeting room is fine.

The office dogs carry documents between desks and get a treat for each delivery:
